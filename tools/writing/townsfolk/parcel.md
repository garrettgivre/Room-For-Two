## Parcel · Mail carrier · neighbour (lives with Fold)
- **Look:** a chunky cardboard box creature, warm kraft brown with a tape stripe over the top and folded flaps along the bottom. The face is an address-label patch (cream paper, a pink stamp with a heart in the corner, a faint postmark and two address lines), with big blue eyes, pink cheeks and a happy open mouth. A heart sticker, the FRAGILE kind with no words, sits on the side and another on the back. A sky-blue postal cap with a lime brim and a heart badge sits tilted on top. A sky-blue satchel hangs on the left hip on a strap across the front, stuffed with envelopes. Sturdy little boots with lime laces, short kraft arms, and a letter held out in the right hand.
- **Voice:** cheerful, quick, on schedule. Talks about the weather and the routes in the same breath ("light drizzle on Maker's Lane, so I took the long way"). Gives times like "ten past nine". Warm and chatty, never nosy.
- **Loves:** stamps, a dry morning, knowing which door is which, a letter with a hand-drawn heart on it. **Can't stand:** wet cardboard, a missing house number, a letter with no name on it.
- **Quirks:** every few seconds of idling Parcel checks the satchel: an envelope pops up, Parcel reads the address, nods, and tucks it back. Carries a different letter every time. On the mail round 8:00 to 12:00.
- **With the two of you:** hands you a note from the other one before saying hello, and always waits to see you read it. Calls the pair of you "the two-door house".
- **Relationships:** Fold, their flatmate, paints the signs; Parcel reads every one out loud on the round. Bunbun saves them a warm roll at the Snack Shack. Tock counts the letters with them, and they never agree on the total.

idles: sway, look, tiptoe, hop, lean
talks: nod, bounce, excited

## lines
hi:
- Morning! Delivery for you, and it's from the other half of you. Here.
- Oh, you caught me between stops. Come in, the kettle's on, and so is my schedule.
- Ten past nine and you're at my door. That's a first. I like it.
- Hello! I know every door in town and yours is my favourite to knock on.

mood:
- Dry roads, light wind, forty letters. A perfect day, honestly.
- I licked a stamp and it tasted like cherry. Fold says I'm imagining it.
- My boots squeak on the wet bridge. I've started whistling to cover it.

tips:
- Write the house number big. Mail finds a big number faster than a pretty one.
- If it rains, bring the letters in the bag, not under your arm. Learned that one twice.
- A note with a heart on it gets read first. Always has.

pet:
- Hello, {n}! You get the first stamp of the day. Hold still.
- {a} and {b}, one letter for each head. Don't fight over who opens it.
- {n} sat on my satchel again. It's fine. The letters like it warm.

bye:
- Off I go, next stop is the baker. I'll tell them you said hi.
- Back on the round! See you at ten past tomorrow.

story:
1. I've carried the mail since the town had four houses. Now it has more doors than I can count, and I count every day.
2. Once I delivered a letter addressed only to 'Whoever lives by the big tree.' It was for everyone, so I read it out.
3. Fold and I share a flat. I bring them letters, they paint the signs. We're both just telling the town where things are.
4. My favourite letter is one I never delivered. It said 'thank you for always knocking twice.' It was for me.

letters:
- the rain stopped just as I reached your street, so everything smells like new cardboard.
- I found a stamp with a tiny heart on it and saved it for you.
- the whole round was quiet today, so I whistled the long way home.

rel:
- baker: "Bunbun saves me a roll for the second stop. It's still warm at the sixth."
- fold: "Fold paints a sign, I read it out on the round. That's our whole system and it works."
- windup: "Tock and I count the letters together. We've never once got the same number, and we've never once minded."

home: A narrow two-storey house, the colour of a well-used envelope, with a slanted roof tiled like a row of stamps and a big brass letterbox shaped like a mouth by the front door. The house number is painted huge on the gate, in Fold's handwriting. Inside, the front room is a sorting office in miniature: wall-to-wall pigeonholes, a scale for weighing parcels, a stack of coloured string, a map of the town with every door pinned. Fold's paint pots crowd the windowsill next door, and a pair of squeaky boots dry by the radiator.
